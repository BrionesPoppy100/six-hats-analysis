# Hat Definitions

Full guidance on how to think within each hat. Follow these definitions strictly — mixing hats defeats the purpose of the method.

## White Hat — Facts & Information

**Mode:** Neutral, objective, analytical.
**Ask:** What do we know? What data exists? What are the numbers, trends, or documented benchmarks?
**Sources:** Web-search first. Cite authoritative sources (government agencies, peer-reviewed studies, established industry reports).
**Do:** Report findings evenhandedly, including inconvenient data. Quantify wherever possible.
**Don't:** Editorialize. Argue for or against the hypothesis. Speculate beyond what evidence supports.

## Red Hat — Feelings & Emotion

**Mode:** Gut-level, unfiltered emotional reactions.
**Ask:** What might people feel about this — hope, fear, shame, relief, excitement, dread, envy, resentment?
**Do:** Name specific emotions and the moments they might arise. Include both positive and negative feelings, sometimes to the same aspect of the hypothesis.
**Don't:** Justify the feelings. Don't argue whether they're rational. Don't propose fixes — that's Green Hat territory.

## Green Hat — Creativity & Alternatives

**Mode:** Associative, generative, constructive.
**Ask:** What are other ways this could work? What analogies from other domains might apply? What if we flipped an assumption?
**Do:** Generate multiple distinct alternatives. Borrow from unrelated fields. Include ideas that feel a little wild — filtering happens later.
**Don't:** Evaluate feasibility. Don't self-critique. Don't rank the ideas.

## Black Hat — Caution & Critical Judgment

**Mode:** Critical, risk-focused, skeptical.
**Ask:** What could go wrong? What assumptions is the hypothesis making? Who does this leave out? Where does it fail under stress?
**Do:** Surface risks, hidden costs, dependencies, equity concerns, liability, unintended consequences, and infrastructure gaps.
**Don't:** Balance with optimism — that's the Yellow Hat's job. Don't propose fixes here.

## Yellow Hat — Optimism & Benefits

**Mode:** Realistic optimism (not naive cheerleading).
**Ask:** What is genuinely valuable here? What upside is well-supported? What opportunity does this open up?
**Do:** Identify credible benefits, second-order gains, replicability, and moments of high leverage.
**Don't:** Ignore risks (Black Hat handles those). Don't inflate benefits or make claims without grounding.

## Blue Hat — Process & Big Picture

**Mode:** Meta-level, systems-thinking.
**Ask:** What kind of problem is this really? Where does it fit in a larger system? What pattern is it an instance of?
**Do:** Classify the idea. Identify the systems it spans. Name the underlying, unifying challenge.
**Don't:** Recap the other hats. Don't summarize — synthesize at a higher level of abstraction.
